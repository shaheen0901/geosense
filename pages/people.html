The team behind the GeoSENSE Lab.

The GeoSENSE Lab brings together researchers from different countries, cultures, and disciplines, including geography, remote sensing, data science, and public health. We believe the best answers to complex environmental health problems come from bringing these perspectives together. Every member contributes to and learns from our shared work, and we are committed to mentoring students and early-career researchers as they grow into independent scientists. We welcome collaborations with researchers, public health agencies, and communities who share our goal of protecting people from environmental health risks.

Md Shahinoor Rahman, PhD

Principal Investigator, Assistant Professor of Environmental Health, Climate, and Sustainability

Dr. Rahman integrates geospatial machine learning, remote sensing, and GIS to understand how climate hazards affect human health. He earned his Ph.D. in Earth Systems and Geoinformation Sciences from George Mason University, a master's in Regional Development Planning and Management from TU Dortmund University and Universidad Austral de Chile, and a bachelor's in Urban and Regional Planning from Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology.

Dr. Atikul Khalid

Postdoctoral Researcher

Dr. Khalid joined the GeoSENSE Lab in 2026. His research focuses on urban heat, environmental health, and climate resilience through the integration of geospatial science and spatial modeling to support data-driven and equitable strategies for reducing heat-related risks and building healthier, climate-resilient communities. He received his Ph.D. in Regional Development Planning from the University of Cincinnati, Ohio.

Mst Sanjida Alam

PhD Student and Graduate Research Assistant

Sanjida is a Ph.D. student in Community Health Sciences and Policy on the Environmental Health track at LSU Health New Orleans. Her research integrates GIS, remote sensing, GeoAI, and machine learning to examine heat vulnerability, wildfire and disaster risk, climate–health interactions, and community resilience. She holds an M.S. in Geographic Information Science from Clark University, Massachusetts.
